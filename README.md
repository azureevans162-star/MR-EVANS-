CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION TO PHYSICS

Physics is the branch of science that studies matter, energy, motion, forces, and how they interact.

Importance of Physics

1. It helps us understand natural phenomena.
2. It supports the development of electrical appliances.
3. It is used in medicine and communication.
4. It helps engineers design machines and buildings.

Physical Quantities

A physical quantity is something that can be measured and expressed with a number and a unit.

Quantity| SI unit| Symbol
Length| metre| m
Mass| kilogram| kg
Time| second| s
Temperature| kelvin| K
Electric current| ampere| A

Measuring Instruments

- Metre rule: measures length.
- Stopwatch: measures time.
- Beam balance: measures mass.
- Thermometer: measures temperature.
- Measuring cylinder: measures liquid volume.

---

CHAPTER 2: MOTION

Motion occurs when an object's position changes with time relative to a reference point.

Important Definitions

Distance: The total length of the path travelled.

Displacement: The change in position in a specified direction.

Speed: Distance travelled per unit time.

Velocity: Displacement per unit time in a specified direction.

Acceleration: The rate of change of velocity with time.

Important Formulae

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time

Acceleration = (Final velocity − Initial velocity) ÷ Time

Using symbols:

v = d/t

a = (v − u)/t

Where:

- u = initial velocity
- v = final velocity
- a = acceleration
- t = time
- d = distance or displacement, as appropriate

Worked Example

A student travels 100 m in 20 s. Calculate the average speed.

Step 1: Write the formula.

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Step 2: Substitute the values.

Speed = 100 ÷ 20

Answer: 5 m/s

Practice Question

A car travels 240 m in 12 s. Calculate its average speed.

CHAPTER 3: FORCE AND NEWTON'S LAWS

A force is a push or pull that can change an object's motion, direction, or shape.

The SI unit of force is the newton (N).

Newton's Laws of Motion

First law: An object remains at rest or continues moving at constant velocity unless acted upon by a resultant external force.

Second law: The resultant force equals the rate of change of momentum. For constant mass:

F = ma

Third law: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Worked Example

A body of mass 4 kg accelerates at 3 m/s². Find the resultant force.

Formula: F = ma

F = 4 × 3

Answer: 12 N

Weight

Weight is the gravitational force acting on a body.

Weight = mass × gravitational field strength

W = mg

Mass is measured in kilograms; weight is measured in newtons.

Friction

Friction is a force that opposes relative motion or the tendency of surfaces to move against each other.

Friction can help us walk and enable vehicle brakes to work. It can also cause wear and unwanted heating.

Practice Question

A force acts on a 5 kg object and produces an acceleration of 2 m/s². Calculate the force.

CHAPTER 4: WORK, ENERGY AND POWER

Work

Work is done when a force causes displacement in its direction.

Work done = Force × Displacement in the direction of the force

W = Fs

The SI unit is the joule (J).

Energy

Energy is the capacity to do work.

Types include:

- Kinetic energy
- Gravitational potential energy
- Chemical energy
- Electrical energy
- Thermal energy
- Light energy

Kinetic energy:

KE = ½mv²

Gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface:

PE = mgh

Power

Power is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred.

Power = Work done ÷ Time taken

P = W/t

The SI unit is the watt (W).

Worked Example

A machine does 600 J of work in 20 s. Calculate its power.

P = W/t

P = 600 ÷ 20

Answer: 30 W

Practice Question

A 10 kg object moves at 4 m/s. Calculate its kinetic energy.

---

CHAPTER 5: HEAT AND TEMPERATURE

Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference.

Temperature indicates how hot or cold a body is.

Modes of Heat Transfer

1. Conduction: Transfer of heat through a substance without bulk movement of the substance. Common in solids.
2. Convection: Transfer of heat by the bulk movement of fluids, such as liquids and gases.
3. Radiation: Transfer of energy by electromagnetic waves; it does not require a material medium.

Specific Heat Capacity

Specific heat capacity is the heat energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 K.

Formula:

Q = mcΔT

Where:

- Q = heat energy in joules (J)
- m = mass in kilograms (kg)
- c = specific heat capacity in J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹
- ΔT = change in temperature in K or °C

Worked Example

Calculate the heat required to raise the temperature of 2 kg of water by 5°C. Take the specific heat capacity of water as 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.

Q = mcΔT

Q = 2 × 4200 × 5

Answer: 42,000 J

Practice Question

Calculate the heat needed to raise 0.5 kg of a substance with specific heat capacity 800 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ by 10°C.

CHAPTER 6: WAVES

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another without overall transfer of matter.

Types of Waves

Transverse waves: Particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave travel.

Longitudinal waves: Particles vibrate parallel to the direction of wave travel.

Wave Terms

- Amplitude: Maximum displacement from the equilibrium position.
- Wavelength (λ): Distance between two successive points in phase, such as adjacent crests.
- Frequency (f): Number of complete oscillations per second.
- Period (T): Time for one complete oscillation.

Formulae

Wave speed = Frequency × Wavelength

v = fλ

Period = 1 ÷ Frequency

T = 1/f

Frequency = 1 ÷ Period

f = 1/T

Worked Example

A wave has frequency 50 Hz and wavelength 4 m. Calculate its speed.

v = fλ

v = 50 × 4

Answer: 200 m/s

Practice Question

A wave travels at 300 m/s and has frequency 100 Hz. Calculate its wavelength.

---

CHAPTER 7: LIGHT AND OPTICS

Light is a form of electromagnetic radiation that enables us to see.

Reflection

Reflection occurs when light bounces off a surface.

The laws of reflection are:

1. The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.
2. The incident ray, reflected ray, and normal lie in the same plane.

Refraction

Refraction is the change in direction of light as it passes from one medium into another because its speed changes.

Lenses

- Convex lens: Thicker at the centre than at the edges; it converges parallel rays of light.
- Concave lens: Thinner at the centre than at the edges; it diverges parallel rays of light.

Practice Questions

1. State the two laws of reflection.
2. Explain why a pencil partly immersed in water appears bent.
3. Name the type of lens that converges parallel rays.

---

CHAPTER 8: ELECTRICITY

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge.

I = Q/t

Where:

- I = current in amperes (A)
- Q = charge in coulombs (C)
- t = time in seconds (s)

Potential Difference

Potential difference is the work done per unit charge.

V = W/Q

Ohm's Law

At constant temperature and other physical conditions, the current through an ohmic conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it.

V = IR

Where:

- V = potential difference in volts (V)
- I = current in amperes (A)
- R = resistance in ohms (Ω)

Electrical Power

P = VI

Also:

P = I²R

P = V²/R

Worked Example

A resistor of 4 Ω is connected across a 12 V supply. Calculate the current.

Using V = IR:

I = V/R

I = 12 ÷ 4

Answer: 3 A

Practice Questions

1. A charge of 60 C flows in 20 s. Calculate the current.
2. A 6 Ω resistor carries a current of 2 A. Calculate the potential difference.

CHAPTER 9: RADIOACTIVITY

Radioactivity is the spontaneous disintegration of unstable atomic nuclei, accompanied by the emission of radiation.

Types of Radiation

Alpha (α): Consists of helium nuclei. It has relatively low penetrating power and strong ionising ability.

Beta (β): Consists of fast-moving particles, usually electrons or positrons. Its penetrating and ionising powers are intermediate.

Gamma (γ): Electromagnetic radiation with high penetrating power and relatively low ionising ability.

Half-Life

Half-life is the time taken for the number of undecayed radioactive nuclei in a sample, or its activity, to fall to half its initial value.

Safety Precautions

- Minimise exposure time.
- Maximise distance from the source.
- Use appropriate shielding.
- Follow radiation safety instructions.

Practice Questions

1. Define half-life.
2. Which of alpha, beta and gamma has the greatest penetrating power?
3. Give two safety precautions when handling radioactive sources.
